01 · About

Lover of exoplanets and equity.

Hi! I'm Phoebe and I'm a Ph.D. candidate in Astronomy and Astrophysics at Penn State, where I focus on exoplanet modeling and planet formation simulations. My work combines N-body simulations, orbital dynamics, and open-source software to answer questions about how planets form and how we measure their properties.

I'm a passionate advocate for increasing the accessibility of science, whether that means rebuilding a research code in a language anyone can use or connecting undergraduates with their first research project. I deeply value students, teaching, and service just as much as novel research.

02 · Research

Questions I'm exploring

My research asks how planets form and how we can best measure the ones we find. I've approached it through three projects.

EXOZIPPy: exoplanet modeling for everyone

EXOFASTv2 is a prominent exoplanet modeling code, but it is written in the proprietary language IDL, which is a significant barrier for the community. I'm rebuilding it from the ground up in Python to increase accessibility and community involvement. Preliminary benchmarking shows consistent results with at least an order of magnitude speed increase. v1.0.0 release coming soon!

2024 – present · with Jason Eastman, Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics · Code on GitHub

Super-Earth formation and orbital dynamics

I run N-body simulations to investigate how different initial conditions in planet formation, such as the presence of outer giant planets, affect the properties of the inner planets that form. Along the way, I wrote a publicly available addition to the Python package REBOUNDx that calculates gas damping forces on bodies during a simulation.

2020 – present · with Mariah MacDonald, The College of New Jersey · Read the Docs Code Description

Searching for planets around white dwarfs

I analyzed archival white dwarf data from the Cosmic Origins Spectrograph on the Hubble Space Telescope, converting spectra into time-series photometry to search for the brief dimming of a transiting object. Injection-recovery tests showed we would have been able to detect transiting sub-Earth-sized objects around the white dwarfs in our sample.

2013 – 2016 · with John H. Debes, Space Telescope Science Institute

04 · Teaching & Mentoring

Learning is a shared experiment

“Repeatedly using a qualifying word such as 'smart' to describe students forces them to internalize the idea that intelligence is just something you either have or you don’t. Once your students have reached your classroom, this damage has probably already been done. However, it is important to vocally, and enthusiastically, combat these thoughts and establish that yes, anyone and everyone can succeed.”
